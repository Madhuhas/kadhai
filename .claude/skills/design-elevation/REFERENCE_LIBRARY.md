# Reference library

Exemplars to learn from. Borrow the principle each one demonstrates; do not reproduce its look. Choose references that match the job - a dense developer tool has more to learn from Linear than from a marketing site.

## Modern product design

**Stripe** - clarity for complex information.
Lessons: strict type hierarchy, generous whitespace around dense content, one confident accent, documentation treated as a designed product, diagrams that explain rather than decorate.

**Linear** - density without clutter.
Lessons: dark-first neutral ramp, small precise type, hairline borders instead of boxes, keyboard-first interaction, motion that is fast and barely noticeable, nothing ornamental.

**Apple** - focus and restraint.
Lessons: one idea per screen, very large type with tight tracking, enormous margins, product as the hero, materials and depth used sparingly and consistently.

**Airbnb** - warmth and approachability.
Lessons: friendly rounded forms, photography carrying the emotion, clear step-by-step flows, human microcopy, strong card system.

**Vercel / GitHub** - developer tooling.
Lessons: monospace as a design element, high-contrast monochrome with a single accent, status communicated by small coloured marks, information-dense tables that stay readable.

**Notion** - calm, content-first tools.
Lessons: interface that recedes behind content, restrained colour, typography doing almost all the work, generous defaults.

## Historical movements

**Swiss / International Typographic Style** - order.
Lessons: mathematical grids, flush-left ragged-right text, sans-serif type, asymmetric balance, objective photography, hierarchy through size and position alone.

**Bauhaus** - form follows function.
Lessons: geometric primitives, primary colours used deliberately, no ornament, honesty about materials, unity of type and image.

**Dieter Rams / Braun** - "less, but better".
Lessons: good design is unobtrusive, honest, long-lasting, thorough down to the last detail, and involves as little design as possible.

**Editorial and print** - pacing.
Lessons: strong typographic contrast, pull quotes, drop caps, deliberate rhythm between dense and open pages, the baseline grid.

**Information design (Tufte)** - truth in data.
Lessons: maximise data-ink, small multiples, sparklines, avoid chartjunk, show comparisons, integrate words and numbers and graphics.

## Natural and mathematical patterns

- **Golden ratio (1:1.618)** and **Fibonacci** sequences for proportions, splits and spacing scales.
- **Rule of thirds** for placing a focal point.
- **Modular scales** from musical intervals for type.
- **Voronoi and cell patterns** for organic, non-grid backgrounds or data maps.
- **Fractals and self-similarity** - the same radius, spacing and weight logic at every size.
- **Gestalt principles** - proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, figure/ground - explain why grouping works without boxes.

## Proven typography combinations

System-font options first, since they need no download:

| Role | Stack |
| --- | --- |
| UI sans | `system-ui, -apple-system, 'Segoe UI', Roboto, sans-serif` |
| Monospace | `ui-monospace, 'Cascadia Code', Consolas, 'SF Mono', Menlo, monospace` |
| Serif | `Georgia, 'Iowan Old Style', 'Times New Roman', serif` |

Pairings when web fonts are allowed:

- **Inter** (UI) + **JetBrains Mono** (code/data) - neutral, technical.
- **Geist** + **Geist Mono** - crisp developer aesthetic.
- **IBM Plex Sans** + **IBM Plex Mono** - engineered, slightly warm.
- **Fraunces** or **Playfair Display** (display serif) + **Inter** (body) - editorial contrast.
- **Space Grotesk** (headings) + **Inter** (body) - characterful but restrained.
- **Source Serif** (body) + **Source Sans** (UI) - long-form reading.

Rules for pairing: contrast clearly (serif with sans, or one family at very different weights) or do not pair at all; never two similar sans-serifs; two families is almost always enough.

## Palette starting points

- **Warm dark**: near-black with a brown tint, off-white text, one saturated warm accent.
- **Cool dark**: blue-tinted charcoal, soft white text, one electric accent.
- **Paper light**: cream background, ink-brown text, a deep accent - softer than white and black.
- **Monochrome plus one**: full neutral ramp with a single hue reserved for action and status.
- **Terminal**: one hue for everything, hierarchy by brightness alone.
